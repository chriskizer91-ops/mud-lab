## 1. How a real river lays itself out (the logic to build from)

A river that wanders across soft ground (sand, silt, loam) sorts its own bed. The same few shapes repeat, and each
one says something about how deep the water is and how firm the bottom is.

| Shape | Where it forms | Why | Depth | Bed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pool** | The outside of a bend, at and just past its tightest point | The fast water is thrown to the outside and scours | The deepest water: 2 to 4 times the crossing depth | Fine silt and mud settle here at low water: soft |
| **Cut bank** | The bank on the outside of the bend, above the pool | The same scour undercuts it | Drops straight in | Steep or vertical, 1 to 3 m: no way down or up for a truck |
| **Point bar** | The inside of a bend | Slow, shallow water drops what it carries | Slopes gently from dry bar into the water | Coarsest at its upstream head and along the water's edge (gravel, coarse sand: firm); finer at its downstream tail and on top (fine sand, silt: soft) |
| **Riffle** | Between two bends, where the deep line swaps from one bank to the other | The current spreads out over the gap between two pools and drops its coarse load | The shallowest, widest, fastest water | The firmest bed on the river: gravel, or packed sand in a sand river |
| **Mid-channel bar** | Where the channel suddenly widens, or just below a side stream | The water slows and drops sediment in the middle, then splits round it | Two channels of unequal depth: one main, one shallow chute | Firm at the head, soft at the tail |
| **Scour hole and junction bar** | Where a side stream joins | The two currents dig at the meeting point and drop a bar just below it on the side stream's side | A deep hole at the junction, a bar below | Hole: deep. Bar: sand |
| **Backwater, slough, old cut-off** | Still water off the main channel, behind a point bar or in an abandoned bend | No current, so the finest mud and rotting plants collect | Often not deep | The softest ground on the river: silt, clay, peat |

Rules of thumb a river follows, which make a made-up one look right:

- **Riffles and pools alternate.** One riffle about every 5 to 7 channel widths; one full S-bend about every 10 to
  14 widths. A 50 m wide river has a riffle roughly every 250 to 350 m.
- **Narrow means deep, wide means shallow.** The same water has to get through. The tempting short crossing at the
  narrowest spot is the deepest one.
- **A riffle usually runs across the river on a slant,** from the tail of the point bar on one bank to the head of
  the next point bar on the other bank. That slanted line is the ford.
- **A crossing needs a ramp on both banks.** Bars are ramps; cut banks are walls. The riffle is the only place with a
  bar on each side.
- **Firm follows fast.** Where the water runs fast and broken, the fine stuff has been washed away and the bed is
  firm. Where it lies still and dark, the bed is mud.
- **Sand on a bar changes with wetness.** Damp sand by the water's edge is the firmest. Dry sand on top of the bar is
  loose. Saturated fine sand at the downstream tail of a bar, where water seeps up through it, is the kind a wheel
  sinks into.

## 2. What that means for where you can cross

| Spot | Looks like | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The riffle, on its slant** | Wide, pale, broken water between two bends; a sand bar running into it on each bank | The crossing. Shallow and firm if you hold the line; deeper either side of it |
| **Straight across at the narrows** | The shortest gap, smooth dark water | The pool. Too deep: the truck floats, then drowns |
| **The outside of a bend** | A clean vertical bank | A drop into the pool, and no way up the other side |
| **Down the tail of a bar** | Flat wet sand and silt, very inviting | Soft: sinks to the axles before it reaches the water |
| **Across a mid-channel bar** | An island with a little channel one side and a big one the other | The chute is easy; the main channel on the far side is the real crossing, and only at the bar's head |
| **Through a backwater** | A quiet arm with reeds | The worst mud on the map |

Suggested fords on the new river, graded the way the trails are:

1. **An easy ford** on a gravelly riffle, about 30 cm on the line: the Stock SUV makes it if it holds the slant.
2. **A harder ford** across a mid-channel bar in a sand reach: a 35 cm chute, the bar, then 60 to 70 cm of main
   channel with a firm line only off the bar's head. Rock & Mud only; the Stock SUV on its 33s at 12 psi might.
3. **A trap** at a narrow bend: looks like the shortest way, is 2 m deep under a cut bank.

How a player learns to read it, without a tutorial: water colour (pale over sand, dark over depth), a broken surface
on the riffles, bare pale bars against reeds and willows on the soft old ground, and the view from the Heron, from
which the whole pattern of bars and pools shows at once. Scouting the river from the air and then driving it is a
natural pairing for this game.

## 3. Fitting it to Mud Lab's soil

Mud Lab already has what this needs: five soils each from dry to wet, a moisture for every spot, flat water with a
level, water drag on tyres and body, sills (Stock 32 cm, Rock & Mud 56 cm) over which the water lifts the body, the
winch (100 ft) and the strap (46 ft).

| River piece | Soil and moisture | Water over the bed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Riffle crest | Sand with gravel in it, firm (a new firm-wet sand, or sand at the moisture where it packs best) | 0.25 to 0.45 m |
| Point bar head and water's edge | Damp sand: firm | 0 to 0.3 m |
| Point bar top | Dry sand: loose (airing down helps, as on the Hill Climb) | dry |
| Point bar tail | Saturated silt and fine sand: soft | 0 to 0.3 m |
| Run between riffle and pool | Sand | 0.6 to 0.9 m |
| Pool | Silt over clay, fully wet | 1.6 to 2.5 m |
| Backwater | Peat and silt, fully wet | 0.2 to 0.6 m |
| Cut bank | Loam, as steep as the ground mesh allows | drops into the pool |

Things the river asks for that Mud Lab may not have yet (to decide in the Mud Lab session):

- **A firm wet bed.** If wet sand always softens, a riffle can't be the firm line. A gravel fraction, or a moisture
  curve for sand that peaks in firmness when damp, would do it.
- **A current** (optional). Still water makes the right spot a matter of depth and bed only. A sideways push on the
  body where the water is fast and over the sills would make leaving the riffle's line costly in the way it is on a
  real river: the truck is lifted, carried off the bar and set down in the pool. Not needed for a first version.
- **Banks too steep to climb** on the outside of bends. The pond's drive-down bank (build 10) is the precedent.
- **The Heron and the bars.** Ground Truth lets the Heron land on river sandbars and drags its wheels in the
  shallows; that code is in `reference/` here, for when he wants it.

## 4. The deep pond

He wants one you can be fully under water in, and stuck.

- **Shape.** A firm shelf round the edge, 0 to 0.5 m deep for 6 to 10 m, then a drop-off to 2.5 to 3 m. Real ponds
  and quarry lakes have exactly this shelf, and it makes the mistake easy to make: the first lengths feel fine.
- **Bottom.** Soft organic muck (peat and silt, fully wet) half a metre thick over clay.
- **Rules it needs:**
  - the body floats and loses its grip once the water is over the sills (already there);
  - **the engine drowns** when the water reaches its air intake, and stays dead until the truck is out. This is new.
    Suggested heights: the Stock SUV about 0.8 m; the Rock & Mud at its snorkel's mouth, about roof height. So the
    Rock & Mud drives in further and gets stuck deeper;
  - a drowned or floating truck sinks into the muck where it stops.
- **Getting out.** Real: only a pull works. Put trees within the winch's 100 ft of the drop-off on at least one
  side, and firm ground within the strap's 46 ft of the shelf's edge, or the pond is a place nothing comes back from.
  He has said he wants places where even the most built-up SUV has to be pulled out; a place with no way out at all
  is a different thing, so ask him.
- **What it looks like from inside.** The chase camera stays above water. The cockpit view going under needs a
  decision (a tint and a waterline is enough).

## 5. What a bigger map does to the soil's store

From `src/ml-soil.js` and `src/ml-world.js` as of build 14:

- The world is one square, `ML_A={x:0,z:1200,h:768,m:140}`: 1,536 m a side.
- The whole-world layer is kept every 2.56 m: 600 x 600 cells, several arrays, filled while the title is up, and two
  600 x 600 pictures on the graphics card.
- The fine soil (8 cm) is made in 5.12 m squares the first time something touches them; 64 cm blocks in 41 m squares
  as the truck comes near; a 122 m window on the graphics card.

| | Now | 4 times the area | 4 times each way |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Side | 1,536 m | 3,072 m | 6,144 m |
| Whole-world cells | 360,000 | 1,440,000 | 5,760,000 |
| Filling them at the title | as now | about 4 times as long | about 16 times as long: spread it over frames or make it by region |
| The engine's ground detail (it starts afresh every 4,096 m: build 8's seam) | inside one stretch | fits inside one stretch if the world's corner is lined up as build 8 did | a seam falls inside the world again |
| The Heron across it at 75 mph | 45 s | 1.5 minutes | 3 minutes |

- The fine soil only costs where it has been driven, so it does not grow with the map.
- The trees, the big map's picture and the jump list all grow with the area.
- **Keep the present ground where it is.** The trails, the Bog and the Hill Climb were graded pit by pit and he likes
  the balance. Growing the square outwards from the present one (the old map as one quarter, the river valley and
  the deep pond in the new ground) leaves every graded place untouched and lets `ml-same.py` go on proving it.
